Welcome to my portfolio. I am a hardware engineer and researcher specializing in hybrid photonic-electronic computing architectures, with a focus on exploiting the unique properties of light to accelerate integer arithmetic at scale.

Core Research

JANUS is my flagship project — a proof-of-concept heterogeneous 3D system that demonstrates how optical waveguide routing can collapse modular multiplication from hundreds of CMOS cycles into a single photonic crossing. The system combines:

  • Residue Number System (RNS) arithmetic for decomposing 64-bit integers
  • One-hot spatial encoding to trade SNR requirements from 138 dB down to > 0 dB
  • Phase-change memory (GST-467) as non-volatile optical crossbar switching
  • 33 GHz CMOS backend with a 4-stage Garner pipeline for sub-200 ps CRT reconstruction

Key Innovation: By encoding operand residues as waveguide indices rather than optical intensities, JANUS achieves noise-immune integer arithmetic with zero holding power on reconfigurable connections.

OptiBranch — Next Generation

OptiBranch extends JANUS with Gallium Phosphide (GaP) parametric amplification and thin-film lithium niobate (TFLN) integrated optics. The system explores:

  • Quantum-noise-limited phase stability without conventional amplifiers
  • 10-phase encoded slots for higher-dimensional RNS extensions
  • Integrated photonic switches at 1550 nm with femtosecond-scale reconfiguration
  • Predictive thermal drift correction using ΔΣ ADC feedback
Background

My research bridges quantum photonics, integrated silicon photonics, and custom digital design. I have collaborated with leading institutions including:

  • Ligentec (Switzerland) — Si₃N₄ waveguide platform expertise
  • LioniX International (Netherlands) — Photonic integrated circuit prototyping
  • imec (Belgium) — Die-to-die bonding and 3D heterogeneous integration
  • SCL Mohali (India) — CMOS backend fabrication

Vision: Photonic computing is not about replacing electronics—it is about coexistence. Light excels at routing, phase manipulation, and parallelism. Electrons excel at sequential logic and state management. The future belongs to hybrid systems that exploit both.
