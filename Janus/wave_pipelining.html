100 GHz Wave-Pipelining

Concurrent In-Flight Optical Transport, 10.0 ps Cycle Budget, and Two-Phase Time Multiplexing

In conventional electronic architectures, cycle time is bounded by the cumulative sum of logic path delays. In the JANUS wave-pipelined architecture, functional stages along the optical-to-electrical datapath are physically decoupled in space, operating concurrently on successive in-flight optical pulses traveling through the waveguide mesh.

Consequently, the system throughput clock period (T_cycle = 10.0 ps, f_clk = 100 GHz) is not constrained by the cumulative optical propagation delay (~750 ps), but is strictly bounded by the bottleneck single-stage clearance and recovery interval.

Two-Phase Time-Multiplexing Strategy

To conserve optical laser power and eliminate an entire stage of passive splitting, the 10.0 ps clock cycle is time-multiplexed into two discrete 5.0 ps half-phases:

  • Sequential Illumination: At any instantaneous moment (within a single 5 ps phase), only half of the photodetectors (262,144) are actively illuminated. Optical energy is steered sequentially rather than split simultaneously, doubling the delivered optical intensity per receiver.
  • Full Cycle Resolution: Over the complete 10.0 ps clock cycle, all 524,288 active detection events across 32 tiles are resolved.
Pipelining vs. End-to-End Latency Clarification

The 10.0 ps cycle represents the throughput interval (the rate at which successive matrix vectors enter the fabric), while single-computation end-to-end latency accumulates across the physical stack:

1. PCM Beneš Optical Propagation (15 stages):    t_opt  ≈ 750.00 ps
2. Ge/Si SAC²M Photodetector Clearance:          t_PD   ≈   1.52 ps
3. Local Metal Interconnect Delay:               t_wire ≈   1.33 ps
4. CMOS CRT Accumulator & Reconstruction Tree:   t_CRT  ≈ 210.00 ps
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Total Cumulative End-to-End Latency:             T_lat  ≈ 962.85 ps (≈ 963 ps)

This 963 ps latency corresponds to an effective wave-pipelined depth of ≈ 96 in-flight computation stages at 100 GHz, sustaining full throughput without pipeline bubbles.
