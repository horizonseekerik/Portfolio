Architecture Overview

A Constraint-Aware Hybrid Photonic Processor for Exact Bounded Computation

JANUS is a patent-pending (Application No. 202611052791) heterogeneous 3D photonic-electronic processor that reformulates large-scale AI acceleration. Conventional optical matrix accelerators attempt to scale analog amplitude accumulation, which collapses at scale due to an insurmountable Shannon-limited precision requirement (requiring 138.4 dB SNR for a 128×128 INT8 MAC).

JANUS eliminates analog precision collapse entirely by converting arithmetic into a binary spatial localization problem using One-Hot Optical Residue Number System (RNS) routing. Light is routed through 256 spatial waveguides per modulus channel, reducing photodetection to a simple binary threshold decision: "Does waveguide k contain light?"

End-to-End System Pipeline
  • 1. Continuous-Wave Optical Source: External 100 W (+50.0 dBm) 1064 nm Ytterbium-doped fiber laser operating at > 75% wall-plug efficiency with low quantum defect (8.3%).
  • 2. Passive Distribution Tree: 1 : 2¹⁸ passive optical splitting tree (18 cascaded MMI stages) distributing carrier light across 262,144 branches with +4.61 dB detection margin and zero ASE noise.
  • 3. One-Hot Spatial Injection: 1 × 256 active LiTaO₃ Pockels electro-optic routers (50 aJ/switch at 100 GHz) inject optical pulses (τ_FWHM ≈ 3–5 ps) into the designated residue waveguide.
  • 4. Non-Volatile Beneš Permutation: 15-stage dilated Beneš network of Antimony Selenide (Sb₂Se₃) low-loss phase-change switches in a 3D vertical reservoir geometry executing modular multiply-in-place at 0 W static hold power.
  • 5. TIA-Less High-Speed Detection: Interleaved Ge/Si SAC²M avalanche photodetectors (105 GHz bandwidth, gain M = 7) directly coupled to event-driven StrongARM regenerative latches (100 aJ/decision), eliminating milliwatt TIA dissipation.
  • 6. CMOS CRT & PRNS Reconstruction: Polynomial and Parallel Residue Number System (PRNS) diagonalizes 64-bit multiplications into two parallel 8-tile optical streams, reconstructed in 210 ps with deterministic fault invalidation.
