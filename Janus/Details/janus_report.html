Verification Method

Three-mode coupled-mode theory (CMT) simulation — pump, signal, idler — numerically integrated (SciPy solve_ivp, RK45, rtol=1e-9). The threshold formula used was independently cross-validated against a real published measurement before being trusted for this design (SiC microring OPO: formula predicted 2.71 mW, paper measured 2.7 mW).

Stability criterion required both: (1) signal photon number settling to under 5% coefficient of variation, AND (2) under 2% drift between two independent late-time windows. The second, stricter check was added specifically after a looser single-window check produced a false "stable" verdict that a longer integration run exposed as a bug.

Scope of this result: this is a first-principles simulation, not measured hardware. It does not include thermal effects, free-carrier dynamics, or fabrication mismatch beyond a basic phase-error test. Treat as strong directional evidence — real device characterization is still required before committing to fabrication.

2  ·  Why This Configuration — Rejected Paths

2.1  Q = 20,000,000 (the original target)  Rejected

The original target used LIGENTEC's highest advertised platform Q (>2×10⁷). Testing showed this Q is too high, not too low. A single ring at Q=20M, R=10μm — at any pump power from threshold (2.65μW, confirmed by two independent calculation methods) up to 200μW — either self-oscillated or, at the real signal level from the actual seed/split chain, saturated to a fixed output ceiling around −32dBm, regardless of how seed power or split ratio were rearranged. ~15dB short of the real noise floor, and unfixable by tuning.

2.2  R=50μm, Q=1×10⁶ — 40.64dB, the highest gain found  Rejected

Technically passed the stability check at the exact point tested, but rejected for two reasons:

  • Cliff-edge behavior: 2.0mW→3.0mW pump (+50%) jumped gain from 14.6dB to 34.1dB — a 19.3dB swing. Signature of operating beside a bifurcation, not a robust point.
  • 5× higher power cost: 55mW total pump vs. 11–16.5mW for the chosen design.

2.3  Single-ring 45dB claim from a real but misapplied paper  Rejected

arXiv 2209.05589 was cited claiming 45dB gain from ~125–166nW input at 9–11mW pump. Checked directly against the source: the 45dB figure is simulated, not demonstrated (real result: 30dB at 9mW), and the paper states gain saturation onset at 100pW — the claimed input was ~2000× above that point. The real 30dB/9mW figure was instead used only as a literature sanity-check for the CMT model.

2.4  Q = 7×10⁷  Rejected

Exceeds even the best published academic Damascene record anywhere (EPFL, ~3×10⁷) — not supported by any citation and not carried forward.

2.5  "0.5mW solves it via energy conservation"  Rejected

Energy conservation (pump ≥ energy added to signal) is necessary but not sufficient — it does not confirm stable gain dynamics. At Q=20M/R=10μm, 0.5mW passed the energy check but the CMT simulation showed operation ~190× above threshold, in a self-oscillating regime.

3  ·  Noise Floor — Final Verified Number

FigureValueStatus
Real noise floor used (100GHz)−17.2 dBmVerified
JANUS paper's own claimed figure (33GHz)−31.76 dBmOriginal claim
Naive bandwidth-scaling to 100GHz~−27 dBmFirst-pass estimate
Rigorous recalculation, real detector data−17.2 dBmUsed

Derivation: thermal-noise-limited receiver, 50Ω load, T=300K, using the actual responsivity (0.3 A/W at 265GHz) from the real cited paper (Lischke et al., Nature Photonics 2021 — JANUS's own reference [29]). Worse than the paper's original claim, likely because that figure assumed a higher effective transimpedance gain than is achievable at 100GHz — TIA gain and bandwidth trade off directly against each other, a real device constraint.

Rejected Noise Floor Claims

"Noise floor below −40dBm at 100GHz"  Rejected

Likely confusion between noise spectral density (dBm/Hz) and total integrated noise power (dBm) — or between a device's intrinsic bandwidth capability and its actual operating noise floor.

~−46dBm from dark-current shot noise alone  Rejected

Arithmetically correct, but accounted for only one non-dominant noise term. Its own source material stated TIA thermal noise usually dominates — which is exactly the −17.2dBm figure used here.

"265GHz gives a better noise floor than 100GHz"  Rejected

265GHz is the detector's intrinsic response speed, not a noise number. Higher operating bandwidth always costs more noise, never less.

Real, unquantified mitigation: matched-filter / integrate-and-dump receiver design (standard optimal receiver theory) — estimated +2 to +4dB improvement over the brick-wall estimate above. Real technique, not yet built into a simulation to confirm the exact number.

5  ·  Other Resolved Design Questions

5.1  3-Tile GST Stacking — Kept, Not Reduced  Resolved

Initial concern: thermal crosstalk between three vertically stacked active PCM layers during simultaneous writes — the least literature-supported risk in the design. Resolved via write-scheduling, not a materials or geometry change: since JANUS uses a "set once, run many times" model rather than continuous active switching, the fix is a controller-level rule — never write vertically-adjacent tiers simultaneously, insert a cooldown delay between same-position writes across tiers. Original 3-tile design retained.

5.2  3D Bonding Terminology — Clarified

Wafer-to-Wafer (W2W)
Both sides full, undiced wafers at bond time
Die-to-Wafer (D2W)
One side pre-diced, placed on an intact wafer
Die-to-Die (D2D)
JANUS's actual regime — both sides pre-diced

5.3  Laser Count / Splitting Architecture — Finalized

Seed laser → passive split → Si3N4 ring cascade amplification (Section 1) → further passive split → detection. Earlier proposals (large single-stage splits with no amplification, or amplification placed after a wasteful large split) were superseded once real energy-conservation and saturation checks were run against the actual signal chain.
