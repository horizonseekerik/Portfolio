JIR Controller & RRNS

Tile Orchestration, Real-Time Sensor Slope Tracking, and Zero-Overhead Weight Reconfiguration

The JIR (JANUS Interface Representator) is the central tile-management and scheduling controller in the CMOS backend. Operating over periodic τ_JIR = 5 µs monitoring cycles, the JIR manages optical packet ingestion, tile allocation, power gating, and dynamic thermal trend tracking across all 32 optical tiles.

Unlike conventional systems that react only after arithmetic errors corrupt computation, the JIR implements Predictive Redundant RNS (RRNS) Engagement. By continuously monitoring on-die temperature slopes (dT/dt), the JIR dynamically engages RRNS parity channels before thermal phase-drift can cause optical false activations, restoring nominal operation once local temperatures stabilize.

Zero-Overhead Parallel Weight Reprogramming (0.1 ms Window)

A critical advantage of non-volatile Sb₂Se₃ phase-change routing is parallel weight loading without sequential scan-chain delays:

  • Parallel Electro-Thermal Addressing: Up to 1,006,632,960 PCM switches across 32 tiles can be reprogrammed in a single 0.1 ms write window via underlying CMOS driver arrays and vertical Cu–Cu interconnects.
  • Transient Energy Budget: At ≈ 10–50 pJ per switch (Tier 3 conservative upper bound: 10 mJ total), the peak transient electrical power is bounded to 100 W across the 300 mm² die (0.33 W/mm² power density, well below the 1 W/mm² flip-chip threshold).
  • Zero CMOS Pipeline Stall: The 0.1 ms weight-programming window overlaps completely with the final CRT reconstruction backlog from the previous epoch. The CMOS backend is never idle, sustaining an overall operational utilization factor of η = 0.85.
  • Sparse Reconfiguration: For typical fine-tuning workloads updating < 1% of weights (f_Δ < 1%), reconfiguration energy drops to < 0.1 mJ, making update overhead negligible.
