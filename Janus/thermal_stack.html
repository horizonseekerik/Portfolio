3D Z-Axis Thermal Stack

Vertical Thermal Partitioning, Directional Heat Extraction, and Sub-Millimeter Package Architecture

Large-scale photonic accelerators cannot sustain deterministic operation if CMOS logic, optical routing waveguides, and detector arrays share a common planar substrate. Lateral heat diffusion from switching transistors directly perturbs the silicon refractive index (dn/dT ≈ 1.86 × 10⁻⁴ K⁻¹), inducing optical phase drift (Δϕ = (2πL/λ)(dn/dT)ΔT) that destabilizes interferometric transfer matrices.

JANUS resolves this through a vertically partitioned 3D Z-axis stack that thermally decouples the optical layer from the dominant digital heat plane, establishing directional heat extraction with a steady-state thermal safety margin of 17× below the optical phase-instability threshold.

Directional Thermal Extraction & Resistance Network

Thermal decoupling is governed by an engineered asymmetry in Cu–Cu bond densities:

  • Downward CMOS Thermal Bottleneck: The CMOS/SiO₂ boundary features a sparse 10,000 mm⁻² bond density, presenting a high vertical thermal resistance (R_th,down = 0.488 K/W) that prevents CMOS heat from penetrating upward.
  • Perimeter Copper Thermal Shunt: CMOS digital dissipation (q_CMOS ↓) is forced laterally into a continuous low-resistance copper perimeter shunt surrounding the 300 mm² die, routing heat directly to the exterior heat sink while acting as an EMI Faraday cage.
  • Upward Photonic Extraction: The SiPh/HS1 boundary features a dense 900,000 mm⁻² Cu–Cu array (R_th,up = 0.18 K/W), creating an open upward conduit (q_photonic ↑) that pulls optical and detector heat directly into Heat Spreader 1.
Thermal Diffusion Constant & Stability Budget

The time required for heat to diffuse vertically across the 100 µm SiO₂ buffer layer is:

τ_diff = (h_SiO2)² / α_SiO2 = (100 × 10⁻⁶)² / (9.05 × 10⁻⁷ m²/s) ≈ 11.05 ms

This 11.05 ms diffusion time is over 2,200 JIR cycles long (τ_JIR = 5 µs). Consequently, high-frequency CMOS thermal transients are completely attenuated before they can reach the SiPh routing layer. Under sustained full-load operation:
